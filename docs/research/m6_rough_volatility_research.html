1. Mathematical Foundations of Rough Volatility

Traditional models of stochastic volatility (e.g. Heston, SABR) assume volatility is driven by standard Brownian motion, which has paths of Hölder regularity $1/2 - \epsilon$. Empirical studies, however, show that the log-volatility process behaves like a fractional Brownian motion (fBm) with Hurst index $H \in (0, 0.5)$. Since $H < 0.5$, the volatility path is highly non-smooth, presenting a highly singular, jagged behavior which is called rough volatility.

2. The Rough Bergomi (rBergomi) Model

The rBergomi model (Bayer, Friz, Gatheral 2016) is a log-normal fractional Volterra model. The instantaneous variance $V_t$ is defined as:

V_t = xi_0(t) * exp( eta * sqrt(2*H) * Y_t - 0.5 * eta^2 * t^(2*H) )

where $\xi_0(t)$ is the market forward variance curve, $\eta$ is the volatility of volatility, and $Y_t$ is a fractional Riemann-Liouville integral:

Y_t = \int_0^t (t-s)^{H - 1/2} dW_s

with $dW_s$ as the asset's orthogonal or correlated driver.

3. Bennedsen-Lunde-Pakkanen Hybrid Simulation

Simulating the fractional Volterra process is difficult due to the singularity of the kernel $(t-s)^{H-1/2}$ at $s = t$ when $H < 0.5$. The hybrid scheme (Bennedsen, Lunde, Pakkanen 2017) resolves this by decomposing the integral into:

  • Local Part: Integrates the power-law singularity analytically over the most recent time step $[t-\Delta t, t]$:
    Y^L_t = \int_{t-\Delta t}^t (t-s)^{H-1/2} dW_s
  • History Part: Approximates the integral over $[0, t-\Delta t]$ as a Riemann sum using weights $b_j$:
    Y^H_t = \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} b_j \Delta W_{t - j \Delta t}
    b_j = \frac{j^{H+1/2} - (j-1)^{H+1/2}}{H + 1/2}

4. Empirical Hurst Exponent Estimation

In physical time-series of asset volatility, the Hurst exponent can be estimated by analyzing the scaling of the $q$-th moment of log-volatility increments:

E[ |log(sigma_{t+dt}) - log(sigma_t)|^q ] = C_q * dt^(q * H)

Specifically, for quadratic increments ($q=2$):

log( E[ (log(sigma_{t+dt}) - log(sigma_t))^2 ] ) = Constant + 2 * H * log(dt)

We can estimate $H$ by performing a linear regression of the log-variance of increments against the log-lag $\log(dt)$ for lags $dt = 1, 2, \dots, N_{\text{lags}}$.

5. ATM Skew Exploding Power Law

One of the most profound signatures of rough volatility in options markets is the behavior of the ATM implied volatility skew $\psi(T) = \left|\frac{\partial \sigma_{\text{implied}}}{\partial k}(0)\right|$ as a function of time-to-maturity $T$. In standard models (SABR, Heston), the ATM skew remains flat or constant as $T \rightarrow 0$. In rough volatility models, the ATM skew explodes as a power law near the short-term boundary:

psi(T) \propto T^{H - 1/2}

Since $H < 0.5$, $H - 1/2 < 0$, which causes $\lim_{T \rightarrow 0} \psi(T) = \infty$, matching the real-world exploding skew observed in historical index option chains!
